Codex and Scrivener for book writing

Explore Codex as an open-source, self-hosted option for planning and writing books, and consider how its workflow differs from Scrivener.

Codex is in development and runs on your own infrastructure. Check the requirements before installing.

Two different ways to work

Scrivener is an established writing application for organising and drafting long projects. Codex is a self-hosted, open-source writing studio in development, with an emphasis on nonfiction research, citations and preparing a book for print.

What Codex brings together

  • A plan board for acts, chapters and scenes, alongside a manuscript editor.
  • A knowledge base and a References workspace for sources, citations and tracking permissions.
  • Print layout and PDF export, subject to the host requirements.

What to consider before switching

Codex requires you to run and maintain its services, whereas a ready-to-install writing application may be more convenient. It is still in development. Check the documentation and test it with a small project before committing a manuscript; do not assume feature parity or a direct migration path.
